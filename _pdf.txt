1 Overview
Core T ask:The goal of this project is to experience the evolution of 3D reconstruction from
“ideal” to “extreme” conditions, and to understand how modern AI priors are changing the
reconstruction pipeline.
1. Start withideal conditions(dense data) to compare classical multi-view geometry with
modern 3D Foundation Models, and study how data-driven scaling affects reconstruction.
2. Challengeextreme conditions(unknown poses, sparse views) using unposed reconstruc-
tion or monocular 3DGS-SLAM without ground truth poses.
3. ExploreGenerative AIto restore or generate missing visual information, thereby im-
proving sparse-view reconstruction.
Key Concepts:
•3D Reconstruction:3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS).
•3D F oundation Models:Classical SfM (COLMAP) vs. feed-forward geometry models
(VGGT/VGGT-Ω).
•Unposed Reconstruction:Pose estimation, global consistency, optimization, and monoc-
ular 3DGS-SLAM.
•Generative AI:Image and video diffusion priors for sparse-view reconstruction.
Group Size:2 students per group.

5 Implementation Roadmap
5.1 Part 1: High-Fidelity Reconstruction (Initialization Analysis)
Motivation & Goal:Modern 3D Foundation Models are changing the traditional reconstruc-
tion pipeline from geometry-driven optimization toward data-driven feed-forward prediction.
Compare classical SfM, a modern foundation model, and its further scaled version to under-
stand this paradigm shift and the impact of model/data scaling.
Scenario:Given a video of an object or scene captured with dense views (sufficient data).
T echnical Pipeline:
1.Pose & Geometry Initialization:
•Plan A (Traditional):RunCOLMAP[1] to estimate camera poses and a sparse
point cloud.
•Plan B (Foundation Model):UseVGGT[17] to predict camera poses and scene
geometry.
•Plan C (Scaled Foundation Model):UseVGGT-Ω [18] to predict camera poses and
scene geometry.
2.3DGS Optimization:
•Convert the estimated cameras/geometry to the required format and train the scene
using the standard3DGS[9] pipeline.
•Key Analysis:Compare initialization runtime, pose accuracy, 3DGS convergence
speed, and final rendering quality across COLMAP, VGGT, and VGGT-Ω.
•Advanced Exploration (Optional):Interested students may explore other recent 3D
Foundation Models such as:DA3[11],Pi3-X[19], or streaming feed-forward models
such asLingBot-Map[4] andHorizonStream[5].
3
Output:Compare three plans in runtime, pose accuracy, convergence, and novel-view rendering
quality. Discuss how data-driven 3D reconstruction differs from classical multi-view geometry
and what further scaling brings.
5.2 Part 2: Unposed Sparse Reconstruction
Motivation & Goal:Dense observations make reconstruction relatively well-constrained. This
part studies the more challenging setting where only sparse views are available and camera poses
are unknown.
Requirement:ChooseONEof the following options (Option A or Option B).
Scenario:Sub-sample the data from Part 1 to create extremely sparse frames (see Section 6),
withNOcamera poses provided.
Option A: Unposed Sparse Reconstruction (Recommended: i3dgs [15])
•F ull-View Reconstruction:First run i3dgs on the complete input sequence to obtain
a well-observed reconstruction.
•Sparse-View Reconstruction:Run the same pipeline again using the sparse sequence
defined in Section 6, without providing camera poses.
•Key Analysis:Compare full-view and sparse-view reconstruction in pose accuracy, ren-
dering quality, geometry completeness, runtime, and failure cases.
•Challenge:Maintaining reliable pose estimation and global consistency as view overlap
becomes increasingly sparse.
Option B: Monocular 3DGS-SLAM (Recommended: HI-SLAM2 [23] or S3PO-GS
[6])
•System Loop:Use a monocular 3DGS-SLAM system to jointly estimate camera motion
and incrementally reconstruct a Gaussian map.
•Key Components:Focus on the interaction betweenT racking(camera pose estima-
tion) andMapping(Gaussian scene update), together with global consistency and scale
control.
•Tip:HI-SLAM2 requires sufficiently overlapping frames for initialization. For extremely
sparse settings, you may use a short dense sequence at the beginning for initialization and
apply sparse sampling to the remaining frames.
•Challenge:Preventing tracking failure, scale drift, and map degradation under sparse or
long-sequence inputs.
Output:A 3DGS model reconstructed without COLMAP-provided camera poses, together
with evaluation of pose estimation and rendering quality. For both options, explicitly compare
the full-view and sparse-view settings.
5.3 Part 3: Closed-Loop Generative Reconstruction (Research)
Motivation & Goal:Sparse observations may not provide enough information for complete
reconstruction. This part explores how generative priors can provide missing visual evidence
and iteratively improve the reconstructed scene.
Scenario:Build upon the reconstruction framework used in Part 2, or another suitable recon-
struction pipeline of your choice.
4
T echnical Pipeline:
1.View Selection:Select target views where additional observations are needed. A simple
default is to interpolate a viewpoint between two sparse input views.
2.Generative Enhancement:ChooseONEof the following routes:
•Route A – Image Diffusion Restoration:Render the selected novel view and restore
it usingGeoQuery[3]. You may follow the reconstruction–generation pipeline of
BRPO[24].
•Route B – Video Diffusion Prior:UseGSFixer[22] or other models to restore or
generate additional views along a selected trajectory. You may follow the reconstruction–
generation loop ofGenF usion[21].
•Implement another reasonable generative reconstruction strategy based on related
work or your own design.
3.Closed-Loop Reconstruction:Add the generated/restored views back into the recon-
struction pipeline, update the scene, and repeat the view-selection and generation process.
Complete at leasttwo generation–reconstruction rounds.
4.Consistency-Aware Generative F usion (Advanced):Generated views may be vi-
sually plausible but geometrically inconsistent. Design a strategy to detect, down-weight,
reject, or correct inconsistent content, e.g., through confidence weighting, geometric align-
ment, or non-rigid deformation. Refer toBRPO[24] andVideo to world[8].
5.Efficient Video Diffusion (Advanced for Route B):Reduce video-generation latency
using training-free acceleration methods such asT eaCache[13] orSenCache[7].
6.Active / Agentic View Planning (Advanced):Replace fixed view interpolation with
an active or agent-based strategy that selects where to generate next based on the current
reconstruction.
Output:Compare the initial sparse reconstruction with the results after each generation–
reconstruction round. Visualize the selected views, generated/restored observations, and the
corresponding reconstruction improvements, and analyze success and failure cases.
